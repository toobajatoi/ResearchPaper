# Protocol

## Anthropomorphism Speaks English: A Scoping Review of Anthropomorphic Communication Cues in Large Language Models and an Agenda for Cross-Lingual Research

**Document status:** Draft. The author reviewed the verification sample. The primary set is large language model output. OpenAlex abstracts are not finished. Scopus, Web of Science, and the ACM website were not searched. The evidence matrix is partial. No manuscript exists yet.

**Protocol date:** 29 September 2026.

**Article type:** Scoping review.

**Target journal:** *Human-Machine Communication*.

**What this project is:** A review of published research. It does not collect original data from AI systems, run an API experiment, benchmark GPT, Claude, or Gemini, recruit participants, or code a new corpus of model outputs.

The title is provisional. "Anthropomorphism Speaks English" will be kept only if the included studies show a disproportionate concentration on English. If they do not, the title will be changed. The cross-lingual cue framework below is an analytical possibility to be tested against the evidence, not a finding.

**Scope decision, 29 September 2026.** The primary set is studies of large language model output. "Large Language Models" stays in the title. Scripted, rule-based, and pre-LLM conversational agents are outside that set, including when the script names a linguistic cue. They are not deleted from the screening log. Their decision is recorded as out of scope. Ollier, Nißen, and von Wangenheim (PMID 35071147; French *tu/vous* and German *du/Sie*) and Vanderlyn et al. (2021) may be cited in the Background or Discussion as pre-LLM evidence for grammar-dependent cues. They do not count as included studies.

## 1. Purpose

The review will map how research has treated anthropomorphic communication cues in large language model (LLM) outputs. It asks how those cues have been conceptualized, how they have been operationalized and measured, which cue categories appear, which systems have been studied, and which languages and cultural contexts are represented. It will estimate how much of the evidence is English-only, describe the dominant methods, and identify linguistic and cultural gaps. It will also ask whether existing anthropomorphism frameworks account for linguistic differences, and what a future cross-lingual research agenda should examine.

Urdu may be used later as an illustrative case for that agenda. Urdu examples will be presented as empirical findings only if the included literature actually contains them.

## 2. Research questions

These are the starting questions. They will not be changed silently.

**RQ1.** How have anthropomorphic communication cues in large language model outputs been conceptualized and operationalized in existing research?

**RQ2.** What types of anthropomorphic communication cues have been identified in LLM-generated text, and how are they measured?

**RQ3.** What languages, linguistic contexts, cultural contexts, and LLM-based systems are represented in existing research on anthropomorphic communication cues?

**RQ4.** To what extent is existing research concentrated on English-language communication, and what linguistic or cultural gaps does this create?

**RQ5.** What directions can be identified for future cross-lingual research on anthropomorphic communication in LLMs?

**Overlap to approve, not a silent change.** RQ1 and RQ2 both touch operationalization. If you want a cleaner split, RQ1 can stay with definitions and conceptual frameworks, and RQ2 can stay with cue types and measurement. The substance of both questions would remain. No change will be made unless you approve it.

## 3. Methodology decision

### 3.1 Decision

Conduct the review with the current Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) scoping-review guidance, and report it with PRISMA-ScR.

The JBI chapter is the conduct framework. It incorporates the earlier Arksey and O'Malley stages and the Levac, Colquhoun, and O'Brien clarifications, and it is aligned with PRISMA-ScR. Arksey and O'Malley (2005) remain the historical source of the six-stage logic. They are not used on their own, because later guidance is more specific about eligibility (the population–concept–context frame), protocol items, evidence selection, charting, analysis, and presentation.

PRISMA-ScR (Tricco et al., 2018) remains the published reporting standard. The PRISMA website still presents the 2018 checklist as the scoping-review extension. An update is in progress: a 2026 methods preprint reports candidate new reporting items and says they will go to a Delphi survey. That update is not yet a reporting standard, so this review will not treat the preprint as the checklist. The search will be documented with the items in PRISMA-S (Rethlefsen et al., 2021), adapted to a scoping review: full strings, dates, databases, and limits.

This is a scoping review because the aim is to map concepts, measures, languages, systems, and gaps. It is not a systematic review of effectiveness. No meta-analysis is planned. A meta-analysis will be considered only if, after charting, the measures are genuinely comparable. That is not expected.

### 3.3 Stages

1. **Protocol.** This document, plus the search strategy in `02_search_strategy.md`. Registration on the Open Science Framework was not done before screening. Screening began on 29 September 2026. A protocol deposited after that date would be a public record of the plan. It would not be a preregistration, and this review will not apply for the Preregistered badge.
2. **Search.** Primary databases, the journal hand search, then supplementary citation chasing. The exact date of each search will be recorded when it is run. No search date is assigned in advance.
3. **Selection.** Title and abstract, then full text, against the eligibility rules below. Reasons will be recorded for every full-text exclusion.
4. **Charting.** One row per included study in a piloted extraction sheet. Fields are listed in section 7.
5. **Analysis and presentation.** Descriptive mapping, basic qualitative content analysis of conceptual and measurement themes, comparison of cue taxonomies, and maps of language, method, culture, and cross-lingual gaps. Counts will come only from the screening log.
6. **Knowledge-user consultation.** JBI treats engagement of knowledge users as part of good practice. This review is sole-authored, and no consultation stage is planned. That is a limitation, not a hidden extra method. It can be added later if you want it.

### 3.5 Critical appraisal

PRISMA-ScR item 12 (critical appraisal) is optional. This review will not produce risk-of-bias scores or exclude studies for quality. It will chart study design, sample, and stated limitations so that methodological patterns are visible. That charting is description, not a formal appraisal.

## 4. Eligibility

Full criteria will be copied into `03_inclusion_exclusion.md` after you approve this protocol. The rules below are the ones the search is built to serve.

### 4.1 Population, concept, context

| Element | Scope |
| --- | --- |
| Population | Studies of large language model output. |
| Concept | Anthropomorphism, anthropomorphic communication, human-likeness, human-like linguistic behavior, or a closely related construct, where the study analyzes or conceptualizes cues or features in machine-generated text or language. |
| Context | Any language, culture, discipline, or setting. Publications from 1 January 2020 through the date of the final search. No restriction on study design. |

### 4.2 Include

A source is in scope when all of the following hold:

- it concerns large language model output;
- it examines anthropomorphism, anthropomorphic communication, human-likeness, human-like linguistic behavior, or a closely related construct;
- it analyzes or conceptualizes cues or features in machine-generated text or language, or it develops a definition or framework for those cues;
- it gives enough methodological or conceptual detail to chart.

Scholarly journal articles, conference papers, and book chapters can be included. Taxonomies and measurement papers are in scope. A position paper is in scope when it develops a conceptualization that can be charted for RQ1.

### 4.3 Exclude

- Embodied-robot studies in which textual or linguistic communication is not analyzed.
- Voice-only studies in which textual communication cues cannot be examined. Prosody, accent, and voice persona are out of scope unless the paper also analyzes language.
- Visual anthropomorphism with no analysis of language (avatars, faces, images, logos).
- Studies with no LLM or generative language system.
- Papers with no substantive discussion of anthropomorphic communication.
- Editorials, news, blogs, and other items without a scholarly contribution.
- Duplicate publication of the same study. The version of record is preferred. A preprint is retained only when no version of record is available and the preprint can be charted.
- Items without enough accessible information to chart (for example, an abstract with no retrievable full text).

Theses and dissertations are not in the primary search. They can be added later only if citation chasing shows that a relevant study exists nowhere else. That exception needs your approval before it is used.

### 4.4 Borderline categories

These are proposed decisions. They need your approval before screening.

| Borderline case | Proposed decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| User-perception studies that rate "human-likeness" and also discuss which communicative features produced that rating | Include, and tag as perception-plus-cues | They inform conceptualization and operationalization. |
| User-perception studies with only a global human-likeness score and no discussion of linguistic features | Exclude | They do not identify or conceptualize communication cues. |
| Rule-based or retrieval chatbots from 2020 onward, with no generative language model | Exclude from the primary set | The review question is about LLM outputs. System type will still be checked during screening so a mislabeled LLM is not dropped. |
| Papers on honorifics, pronouns, grammatical gender, or politeness in LLM outputs that never use the word "anthropomorphism" | Include only if the paper treats those features as human-like, social, or anthropomorphic communication | This protects RQ2 and the grammar-dependent question without admitting every multilingual NLP paper. |
| Human-versus-machine text detection | Exclude, unless anthropomorphic cues are an object of analysis | Detection accuracy is a different question. |
| Prompt-engineering papers that tell a model to "act human" and do not study cues | Exclude | The instruction is not an analysis of anthropomorphic communication. |
| Health-chatbot papers | Apply the same rules | Clinical setting does not by itself include or exclude a paper. |
| Personality expressed in generated text | Include when the paper treats that wording as human-like or social. Exclude a behaviourist trait score that is not about the wording | DeVrio et al. (2025) already chart personality as a linguistic expression that contributes to anthropomorphism. Applied on 29 September 2026. |

## 5. Search

The reproducible strings, field codes, and source list are in `02_search_strategy.md`.

**Primary sources, revised 29 September 2026:** OpenAlex, searched on title and abstract, as the multidisciplinary index; ACM Digital Library, searched on the public website; IEEE Xplore, searched on the public website; ACL Anthology metadata; hand search of *Human-Machine Communication*. PubMed remains a supplementary health-communication search, not the main index.

**Not searched, and not to be named as sources:** Scopus and Web of Science. They need a subscription this review does not have. Their absence is a limitation. It is not filled by pretending OpenAlex is either database.

**Access-dependent sources not searched:** Communication & Mass Media Complete and PsycINFO.

**Supplementary:** backward and forward citation chasing of included studies, starting with the seed papers; Google Scholar only to locate a known item. Google Scholar hit counts will not be entered as a PRISMA identification total.

**Period:** 1 January 2020 through the date the last search is completed. That date will be written down when the search is run. It is not stated here because the search has not been run.

**Publication language:** No language limit will be applied in the databases. The search terms are in English. That is a limitation for RQ4, because a review of English-language concentration cannot pretend that English-only queries are neutral. The mitigation is a separate sensitivity search for named languages and for linguistic features, described in the search strategy. Records with no English title or abstract may still be missed. That limit will be reported.

*Human-Machine Communication* states on its About page that it is indexed in Scopus, ProQuest, EBSCO, Google Scholar, Informit, and DOAJ (https://stars.library.ucf.edu/hmc/about.html, consulted via search on 29 September 2026). Web of Science coverage was not confirmed from that page and will be checked in the database at search time. The journal will still be hand-searched, issue by issue, because a small journal can contain relevant articles that a keyword query misses.

## 7. Data charting

One row per included study. Proposed columns:

Study ID; citation; year; venue; DOI; URL; database or source; research objective; system or LLM; language of the communication studied; cultural context; study type; dataset or sample; interaction type; anthropomorphism definition; cue category, in the source's own terms; operationalization; measurement method; inclusion reason; findings; limitations stated by the authors; relevance to cross-lingual research.

The inclusion reason records why the study was kept. The findings column records what the study reported. A screening sentence does not go in the findings column. Both stay empty until the full text has been charted, except that an inclusion reason may be copied from the screening log.

Cue labels will be extracted in the words of the source first. Only after that chart exists will the review ask whether the cues support a distinction among:

- language-independent cues (possible examples: first-person self-reference, explicit self-identification, claims of agency, emotional claims);
- grammar-dependent cues (possible examples: grammatical gender, honorifics, pronouns, address forms, morphology);
- culture-dependent cues (possible examples: kinship terms, culturally specific politeness, religious expressions, culturally specific relational language).

A category enters the framework only if the charted literature supports it. If the literature does not support the three-way distinction, the manuscript will say so. Ollier et al. and Vanderlyn et al. are the clearest pre-LLM examples of a grammar-dependent cue. They can be cited in the Background or Discussion. They are not included studies.

4. Language distribution, including English-only versus multilingual or non-English studies.

6. Cultural-context mapping.

Tables will cover year, system, language, English-only versus other, cue categories, methods, cultural contexts, and theoretical frameworks. Empty cells will stay empty. Counts will not be filled in before screening.
